\section{Computational cost}
\label{sec:performance}
% P:performance-01
The cost of a request depends on what it returns, so cost is compared by operation and only where the returned quantities agree within stated tolerances.
Five operation classes are distinguished (Table~\ref{tab:cost}): a property bundle at fixed density; construction of a pressure-specified state; a declared equilibrium solve; explicit phase discovery; and first-order actions on a state that is already solved.
FeOs~0.10.1~\citep{rehnerFeOsOpenSourceFramework2023} and Clapeyron.jl~0.6.29 under Julia~1.13.1~\citep{walkerClapeyronjlExtensibleOpenSource2022} supply the comparator implementations.

% P:performance-01a
A timing comparison requires scaled property differences below $10^{-8}$, relative density-closure differences below $10^{-10}$, relative equilibrium pressure and density differences below $10^{-7}$, and absolute composition and phase-fraction differences below $10^{-8}$.
The scaled property difference is the absolute difference divided by the largest of the two absolute values and one in the reported unit.
All seven operation rows, including prepared saturation, met these criteria in preflight and timed-return comparisons; no numerical comparison was excluded.

% P:performance-01b
Initial model construction, process startup, Julia compilation, warmups and request transport are excluded.
Ordinary propane saturation compiles the equilibrium declaration within each timed call; the prepared row compiles it once before warmups and times a fresh solve and return extraction.
Neither saturation call supplies a previously solved endpoint or density root, and the prepared row uses its own fresh comparator solves.
The central-difference timings include constructing shifted parameter records and mixtures and re-solving the displaced states.

% P:performance-01c
Each code runs in its own persistent worker process, and two independent package workers bracket the FeOs and Clapeyron.jl workers in alternating order over three repetitions.
Each worker's time is the median of its three block medians of warm calls.
Each paired ratio divides a package block median by the comparator block median from the same repetition; the reported ratio is the median of the six such ratios, two package workers by three repetitions, with their full range beside it.
That range describes observed spread, not a confidence interval.
The ratios compare the calls each code exposes for the same result, not equal algorithms, and they hold for one host.

% P:performance-02
The marginal cost of exact derivatives is reported as actions on a solved state: the time of the first-order actions after a central solve, against the time of central differences that re-solve the state at shifted inputs.
The ethanol--butane bubble-point actions return pressure and vapor composition along temperature, the binary interaction parameter and the two ethanol association parameters; the water--pentanol actions return the water mole fraction of each liquid and the water-rich phase amount along temperature and the binary interaction parameter.
That ratio excludes the central solve, which both routes need, so it is not a speedup of a whole calculation or a comparison with another code's implicit derivatives.
No comparator was timed on charged or reacting cases, and no comparative cost claim is made for them.

% P:performance-03
The fixed-density bundle uses propane at 300~K and 500~mol\,m$^{-3}$ and returns pressure, residual molar enthalpy, residual molar isochoric heat capacity, the logarithm of the fugacity coefficient and residual molar Helmholtz energy divided by $RT$.
The separate density-closure measurement times pressure-specified state construction over a changing-pressure sequence of liquid propane states at 300~K near 1.9974~MPa, with elapsed time reported per state.
Declared equilibrium cases are propane saturation at 300~K, equimolar ethanol--butane bubble pressure at 373.15~K, and an equimolar water--pentanol liquid split at 298.15~K and 101.3~kPa.
Explicit discovery uses equimolar methane--ethane at 190~K and 1~MPa, a two-phase point at which all three codes return the same phases.
The discovery ratio applies only to this matched two-phase point and does not extend to other states.

% P:performance-03a
Measurements used an AMD Ryzen 9 5900XT with 32 logical CPUs and a reserved physical core, with serial workers pinned to logical CPU~31 at niceness~10 and one numerical thread each.
The core was reserved from user programs only; system services and the login session remained outside the reservation.
A block was admitted only when the core reservation and worker affinity were in place and no compiler process was observed before or after it.
Observed one-minute load averages of 5.89--7.34 describe the host conditions and were not an admission criterion.

% P:performance-03b
Each worker supplied 75 samples for each property-bundle, declared-equilibrium and prepared-saturation case, 15 for density closure and discovery, and 30 for each exact-action or central-difference set, across three blocks.
The electrolyte calculation was timed for the package only: the eight water/ethanol/isobutanol/NaCl feeds of Section~\ref{sec:cross-code} (Fig.~\ref{fig:electrolyte-lle}a) at 293.15~K and 100~kPa with constant permittivity, with 15 samples per worker.
The first package worker's median was 158.3~ms for the whole eight-feed set, with block medians from 158.3 to 158.9~ms; the second package worker gave 159.4~ms, from 159.1 to 159.5~ms.

% P:performance-03c
Six blocks and 110 samples from an incomplete acquisition with incomplete compiler-process screening were excluded in full.
The selected acquisition contains 114 blocks and 1\,890 samples, with no disturbance retries or numerical exclusions.

% P:performance-04
The property bundle, density closure and water--pentanol split take less time than in FeOs, while all seven matched operations take more time than in Clapeyron.jl throughout their observed paired ranges (Fig.~\ref{fig:cost}).
Density closure takes 2.83 times the Clapeyron.jl time.
Ordinary propane saturation takes 3.61 times the FeOs time and 3.33 times the Clapeyron.jl time; prepared saturation takes 1.56 and 1.43 times, respectively.
The ethanol--butane bubble point takes 2.09 times the FeOs time and 5.35 times the Clapeyron.jl time.
Explicit discovery takes 12.0 times the FeOs time and 25.4 times the Clapeyron.jl time.

% P:performance-04a
These measurements do not isolate the causes: the codes differ in initialization, reuse of previous results, stopping rules and discovery method, and matching the returned state does not establish equivalent phase-search coverage or a package-wide speed factor.
The two solved-state action ratios quantify marginal derivative cost only.
